With a week having passed since our private view event on Friday 30th October, the dust has settled and we're delighted to report the opening was a great success. With over 30 guests attending, looking over the harbour at Whitehaven, we opened the event with speeches from the Beacon Learning and Exhibition Engagement Manager, Alan Gillon, and Exhibitions and Events Trustee, Ian Smith.

Alan spoke about the journey from first contact in 2025 to now. The Beacon is a predominantly local museum, and to be able to share local stories from local people is exactly what they're looking for; Pillar 200 fits that bill.

Ian spoke to the audience about how he picked out the seminal moments from that first ascent of Old West Route to now. 

Ian: _"Climbing used to be dangerous in the 1800s and early 1900s, it was for the select few. Climbing has been democratised by the advent of safety gear, it's still risky, but the risk is reduced, opening up the sport."_

We thought this was really interesting, summing up the exhibition. As we moved from the wealthy upper-class climbers of the 1900s, the protection increased, the access grew, and now climbing was for everyone. With the increase in safety gear, any curious mountaineer can tackle a climb without peril. 

![](/uploads/dsc05203-x.jpg)

With climbers such as Mabel Barker and Siegfried Herford climbing with little more than some snappable hemp rope and hobnail boots, the risks were so extreme. Climbing a diff could be more risky than those currently tackling a 8b+ sport climb, given there's no room for error. This meant only the boldest of climbers could even get off the ground.

With the advent of safety gear, that risk shunts up the grades, and only once those E grades are reached, with sparse protection and difficult moves, with sticky shoes, harnesses and helmets, could we even begin to try and compare the risk.

This leaves room in the lower grades for those with slightly more self-preservation to be able to head to the crag. Whilst climbing is always dangerous, the risk is minimised, and more can be involved.

If you head to the exhibition, you'll be able to see the full progression of this protection, from piton to prototype 2027 gear.
