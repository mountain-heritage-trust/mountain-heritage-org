After a successful micro-exhibit at Keswick Museum, we've ramped everything up for a full exhibition on the 4th floor of the Beacon Museum in Whitehaven, its first-ever mountaineering exhibition.

Ian has spent months working with other knowledgeable climbing historians to weave a narrative of climbing from the first ascent of Pillar's Old West Route in 1826 to the present day.

Being a guidebook writer and climbing magazine editor, Ian is no stranger to climbing knowledge, but this task was slightly more monumental. It is more about what not to include than what was included. Ian reflected:

"There was a huge amount of discussion with the other trustees about what we would need to include to create the most compelling narrative, whilst choosing what may need to wait for another day." 

This makes the case for future exhibitions that explore topics we only touch on: sea cliff climbing, sport climbing, comp climbing, the interwar period, and more.

The 3D objects are another consideration. We have a huge collection of climbing equipment at the Trust, and we are honoured to have some of Stephen Reid, Fred Hall and Trevor Langhorne's personal collections. This has provided us with a comprehensive history of climbing equipment from the first peg to a prototype DMM Dragon 3.

With months, arguably years, of work in this exhibition, we are so excited about the opening, almost as excited as Ian when the Boreal Ninja's arrived in the 11th hour!

This exhibition has been made possible with support from the BMC, UKClimbing, DMM, Needle Sport, The Climbers Shop, Joe Brown Shops, Beta Climbing Design, Mountain Rescue England and Wales (MREW), The Fell and Rock Climbing Club and the National Heritage Lottery Fund.

Cockermouth MRT, the team covering Pillar, have contributed to the exhibition, showcasing the important work they do in the surrounding hills. Without MREW, safe venture into the fells would not be possible.
